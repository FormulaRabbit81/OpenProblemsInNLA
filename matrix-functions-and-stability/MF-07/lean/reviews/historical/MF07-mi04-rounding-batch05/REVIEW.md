## Mathematical checks

**Actual norm and compact maximization.** `IsComplexNorm` contains ordinary nonnegativity, definiteness, triangle and complex-homogeneity conditions. It contains neither a supplied basis nor a desired growth estimate. NormGeometry derives continuity from the established upper comparison and derives compactness of the unit ball as a closed subset of the Euclidean closed unit ball. Auerbach then maximizes the continuous absolute determinant over the whole finite product of this compact ball. The zero vector proves nonemptiness. Columns `(1/c)e_i`, where `c ≥ 1`, form an explicit feasible matrix with nonzero determinant, so the actual maximizer has nonzero determinant. Neither compact attainment nor invertibility is assumed.

**Coordinate bounds and inverses.** Replacing column `i` of the maximizing matrix by the normalized vector `Tz/v(Tz)` gives the determinant formula `z_i det(T)/v(Tz)` and hence `|z_i| ≤ v(Tz)`. The proof separately handles `v(Tz)=0`: definiteness gives `Tz=0`, and the actual nonsingular matrix inverse gives `z=0`. It never divides by a possibly zero norm or determinant. Triangle inequality and the unit-ball column bounds give `v(Tz) ≤ Σ_i |z_i|`. The two finite Cauchy–Schwarz bounds then yield

`‖z‖ ≤ sqrt(d) v(Tz)` and `v(Tz) ≤ sqrt(d) ‖z‖`.

The proof needs columns of norm at most one, not the manuscript's additional observation that maximizing columns have norm exactly one. Omitting that unused equality weakens no required conclusion.

**Ordered singular coordinates are constructed.** SingularCoordinates applies Mathlib's actual decreasing eigenvalue/eigenvector enumeration to the Hermitian matrix `H = T Tᴴ`. The chosen orthonormal basis yields a unitary matrix `Q` with `H Q = Q diag(λ)`. Invertibility of `T` proves positive definiteness of `H`; unitary conjugation then proves every actual listed `λ_i` positive. Thus `σ_i = sqrt(λ_i)` is positive and nonincreasing. The proof does not assume that an unrelated reindexing of eigenvalues preserves order.

It explicitly defines `R = Tᴴ Q D⁻¹`, proves `T R = Q D`, proves `RᴴR=1`, and obtains `RRᴴ=1` by the finite square-matrix inverse identity. All diagonal inverses are justified by strict positivity. This is a genuine factorization, not an SVD hypothesis or an unproved choice of singular values. Repeated eigenvalues and equal singular weights are retained.

**Transport and the exact factor d.** RoundedNorm sets `P=QD`, `U=D⁻¹Qᴴ` and `w(x)=sqrt(d) v(Px)`. It proves both `UP=1` and `PU=1`, then proves all norm axioms of `w`. Since `P=TR` and `R` is unitary, the two coordinate bounds give exactly `‖x‖ ≤ w(x) ≤ d‖x‖`. The identity `P rotatedGenerator(Q,σ,A)=AP` transports the already constructed approximate norm's generator inequality with the same `a>1`. The entry bound uses the actual formula

`rotatedGenerator(Q,σ,A) = D⁻¹ (Qᴴ A Q) D`

and unitary invariance of the Euclidean operator norm, giving the required orientation `L σ_j/σ_i`. The value `L` is the actual `familyNorm M`; its domination of each generator is obtained from the compact-maximum theorem rather than supplied as a new final premise.

**Edges and nonvacuity.** The rounded export assumes precisely `1 ≤ d`; dimension one is retained, and `sqrt(d)>0` is derived before it is used. The helpers that also quantify over dimension zero have no unjustified nonempty-index premise: finite empty sums and the order-zero determinant remain meaningful, and positivity of a nonexistent weight is vacuous only in these auxiliary zero-dimensional statements. Zero vectors are treated explicitly. Individual generators may be zero or singular; invertibility is required only of the basis matrix that the proof itself constructs. Equal weights require no strict gap and introduce no division by a difference. The imported identity-family proof and actual product-envelope construction supply nontrivial consumers/witnesses for the radius-one and norm predicates.
