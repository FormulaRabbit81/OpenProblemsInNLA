## Statement and mathematical correspondence

The final theorem retains the permanent SF-01 target: every real matrix of every dimension `n >= 1`, with the original spectral H-matrix property and strictly positive diagonal, starts from `X0 = A` and obeys exactly `X(k+1) = (Xk + Xk^-1 * A)/2`. For every `k >= 0`, it proves the H-matrix property, positive diagonal and actual invertibility. The multiplication order is correct. No convergence, scaled initialization or Halley extension is claimed.

`IsSpectralM` uses a representation `C = s I - B`, entrywise nonnegative `B`, and a strict bound on the actual full complex algebraic spectral radius. The radius is the supremum of complex eigenvalue moduli, not an entrywise matrix norm or a real-spectrum substitute. The proof establishes finiteness, nonemptiness and attainment in positive dimensions before using this supremum. The hypothesis is nonvacuous; for example `A = I` has comparison matrix `I`, with the spectral witness `s = 1`, `B = 0` and radius zero. No proof-carrying data structure assumes the intended Newton conclusion.

The two directions of the spectral/positive-weight bridge are supplied. Along `s I - t B`, `0 <= t <= 1`, unitness follows from the strict complex spectral bound. Continuity of the actual inverse applied to the all-ones vector, the positive endpoint at zero, and the exclusion of a nonnegative zero-coordinate boundary give positive weights at `t = 1`. Conversely, a weighted Z-matrix yields `s = max diag(C)` and `B = s I - C >= 0`; the strictly smaller maximum weighted row ratio bounds every complex eigenvalue through a maximum component of a genuine complex eigenvector. These arguments do not assume the equivalence they are intended to prove.

The comparison-weight argument proves actual unitness of `A` by strict diagonal dominance after positive column scaling. Nonnegative shifts preserve the comparison identity and admissibility. The inverse comparison follows from the entrywise absolute-value inequality and the reused weighted maximum principle applied to actual inverse columns. Hence all inverse cancellations used later have their unit prerequisites. In particular, totalized Lean inverses at singular matrices are never used to make the final result hold spuriously.

For valid finite scalar ridge data, `a,b > 0`, positive poles and nonnegative weights, the ridge evaluated on the comparison matrix is a weighted Z-matrix with strict positive action. Resolvent domination controls every diagonal and off-diagonal entry of the ridge at `A`, proving the original H-matrix predicate, positive diagonal and unitness. The comparison direction is correct, and arbitrary nonnegative weights are retained.

The finite pole matrix has quadratic energy equal to a positive diagonal contribution from the old poles plus a nonnegative rank-one contribution. If all ordinary coordinates vanish, the distinguished coordinate contributes strictly because `sqrt(a) > 0`; otherwise the old positive pole terms supply strictness. This covers an empty old pole family and zero weights. The real Hermitian spectral theorem then provides a genuine orthogonal diagonalization with positive eigenvalues. Repeated eigenvalues and zero resulting residues are permitted; no simple-spectrum or nonzero-residue assumption appears. Solving the pole matrix against its distinguished-coordinate vector gives the exact residue normalization, with every divided scalar proved nonzero where required.

The scalar-to-matrix step is a real matrix identity. Generic block and rectangular-lift algebra identifies the base and pole blocks with actual shifted matrices, establishes their units, and diagonalizes the pole-block inverse. The compressed factor satisfies `ridgeEval = A * factor`; its inverse is therefore exactly `ridgeEval^-1 * A`. The rank-update calculation retains noncommutative multiplication order throughout, and its compression plus the residue normalization yields the full matrix reciprocal identity. There is no unsupported substitution of a scalar identity into a nonsymmetric matrix and no circular use of the desired Newton preservation theorem.

The next ridge data are selected from scalar pole data before quantifying over the dimension and input matrix.
