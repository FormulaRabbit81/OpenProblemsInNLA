## Correctness and faithfulness

I tried the usual failure boundaries: `p=1`, nonintegral and arbitrarily large `p`, the infinity endpoint, dimension one, repeated eigenvalues, zero eigenvalues in the auxiliary PSD and arbitrary-matrix semantics, and scalar zero in homogeneity. The original target requires positive definite input matrices; zero and singular inputs are not falsely advertised for that target. The stronger auxiliary PSD lemmas do cover their singular and zero cases.

`finiteSchattenNorm` is the real power of the real trace of the positive CFC modulus power. `FiniteSemantics` proves its identity with the actual Mathlib singular-value sum for arbitrary complex square matrices. It uses the sorted Gram spectrum with multiplicities, composition only at nonnegative exponents when the Gram matrix may be singular, and the square-root/rpow identity including eigenvalue zero. `OperatorNorm` identifies the infinity formula with the actual complex Euclidean-map operator norm and then the first singular value. It explicitly activates the L2 matrix norm before using C-star norm lemmas. No entrywise or function-space matrix norm is substituted.

The substantive proof route was checked as follows:

1. `SpectralPowers`, `GeometricCongruence`, `MeanPositivity`, and `HalfFixedPoint` prove the positive square-root and Riccati facts for the literal means. The fixed-point and explicit square-root formulas for the normalized half-power means are consequences, not defining axioms. All eight matrices later fed to the positive-cone norm lemmas are actually proved positive definite. Invertible congruence is genuinely arbitrary.
2. `PolarPowers`, `OrderPowers`, `FurutaBase`, and `FurutaHalfPower` prove the required Furuta slice. The base exponent `(1-s)/(s+t)` is shown to lie in `[-1,0]`, and the negative-power comparison has the correct reversed direction. The finite extension uses `A₁=A²`, `B₁=(A^(1/2) B² A^(1/2))^(2/3)`, then a Loewner–Heinz exponent explicitly in `[0,1]`. Denominators and invertibility are established. The range `r∈[0,1]` includes the needed `r=1/(2p-1)` for every `p≥1`. The argument matches the relevant Fujii proof rather than assuming the paper's theorem.
3. `HeronOrder` and `HeronNorm` derive the weighted-source operator-norm comparison through a positive normalization of the target. The target norm is proved strictly positive using `n≥1`; no zero norm is divided by. The scaling is by `c²` in the matrix input and by `c` in the weighted source/target.
4. `CompoundAlgebra`, `DiagonalPowers`, `CompoundSpectral`, and `CompoundNorm` construct actual minors and identify them with exterior-power matrices. Multiplication, adjoints, unitaries, diagonal products, positivity, and all real powers follow from those definitions. Sorting is not an assumption: `spectrumIndexEquiv` relates the arbitrary eigenbasis index to `eigenvalues₀`, and its antitone property comes from the pinned Mathlib API. The maximum product over a k-subset is bounded by the first k sorted entries, and an actual subset attaining it is constructed. The empty compound degree is included; for `k≤n`, the compound dimension is proved positive.
5. `ScalarLogMajorization` is a proved finite Abel-summation argument, not an imported log-majorization oracle. Prefix products give prefix sums of logarithmic differences at every k. Positive descending source weights give a nonpositive weighted total. The scalar tangent inequality converts that to the required sum comparison. The target vector need not be sorted; the source is explicitly antitone. Dimension zero is handled in the induction. `WeightedTrace` transports this result through every actual compound degree and explicit cyclic trace identities.
6. `TraceSpectral`, `TraceOverlap`, `TraceYoung`, and `HeronTrace` use actual spectral decompositions and squared complex overlap entries. Both overlap marginals equal one. Weighted scalar AM–GM gives the trace bound; the fixed point then cancels a strictly positive Young weight for all real `p≥1`. This follows Dinh–Dumitru–Franco's argument with the required prerequisites proved locally.
7. `PowerScaling`, `PositivePowers`, `TraceHolder`, and `PositiveSchatten` establish homogeneity, PSD order monotonicity, and the PSD triangle inequality for that same finite Schatten formula. `p=1` is treated by trace linearity; `p>1` uses the actual conjugate exponent. The finite Holder proof preserves zero overlap weights. Cancellation separates the zero-trace case and proves the canceled factor positive in the other case.
8. `HeronFinite` converts the proved trace inequality to the finite norm comparison. `HeronInfinity` proves the infinity case by a finite-power contradiction using `||U||^m ≤ tr(U^m) ≤ n||U||^m` and an unbounded power of a ratio greater than one. It neither samples finitely many p nor assumes an unproved limiting theorem.
9. `PolarModulus` proves the positive-square identity behind `W+W* ≤ |W*|+|W|`. `LinComparison` congruences it by the correct A square root and checks both Gram products with noncommuting factors in their actual order. `Complement` uses `2 middle = Heron endpoint + right endpoint`, the proved Heron and Lin comparisons, and the genuine norm inequalities to obtain both final conclusions. `schattenComplementConjecture` packages exactly those universal statements.

No missing mathematical hypothesis or surrogate target was found. This is a source-level mathematical assessment; mathematical meaning remains distinct from the kernel's proof validation.
