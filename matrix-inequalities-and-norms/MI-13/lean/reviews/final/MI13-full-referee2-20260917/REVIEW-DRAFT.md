## Mathematical assessment

The target is the retained Nobori complex rectangular inequality for all natural `m,n ≥ 2`, `A,C : Matrix (Fin m) (Fin n) ℂ` and `B : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin m) ℂ`, with literal coefficient two. The canonical declaration has no invertibility, rank, real-entry, distinct-spectrum, size, or unproved analytic premise. `spectralNorm` is the norm of the actual Euclidean CLM, not Lean's default entrywise matrix norm. `frobeniusNorm` flattens every entry into the genuine complex Euclidean norm. Its square is separately proved equal to both the complete entry energy and the real Gram trace. `singularValue` directly calls Mathlib's decreasing, nonnegative singular-value sequence of that same linear map, including zero extension.

The singular-value foundation uses the actual sorted Gram eigenbasis; the Mathlib source inspected explicitly states multiplicities and descending order. Positive normalized images are proved orthonormal from the adjoint Gram equation. Zero singular images are handled separately before extending only the positive family to an orthonormal basis. Thus the SVD does not normalize zero vectors, silently assume full rank, or require distinct singular values. Empty dimensions are retained in the helper statements. The matrix SVD follows by coordinate evaluation and the proved unitary basis matrices.

The operator norm proof gives the image energy in the complete ordered Gram basis. It proves the upper bound coefficient by coefficient and attains it at the zeroth Gram basis vector when the domain is nonempty; the empty-domain case is separate. Norm definiteness then identifies norm zero with the zero matrix. No nonzero-domain instance is global. Unitary invariance is established through actual Gram characteristic polynomials and ordered eigenvalues, preserving multiplicities and zero extension, and through trace cyclicity for Frobenius norm.

The Hilbert–Schmidt commutator is represented by a concrete matrix of coefficients on the entry space; its action is proved to be the actual commutator. Trace cyclicity proves the adjoint is the commutator by `X*` and that the Gram quadratic form is the actual squared commutator norm. The conjugate-linear map `J(Y)=[X*,Y*]` has `J²=-T`, commutes with `T`, is orthogonal to `Y` in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, and has the correct norm. These signs and conjugations are important and are correct.

The top eigenvalue is obtained from the finite-dimensional Rayleigh supremum of the actual Gram operator. The corresponding vector is nonzero, the eigenvalue is nonnegative, and its upper bound holds for every matrix; the zero test prevents division by zero. For a positive eigenvalue, `Y` and `JY` are nonzero orthogonal eigenvectors with that same real eigenvalue. For any complex-linear functional, either `Y` already lies in its kernel or `f(Y)JY-f(JY)Y` is a nonzero kernel eigenvector. The proof of nonzeroness uses actual inner-product definiteness, not an assumed multiplicity theorem.

The SVD corner functional evaluates the actual transformed commutator entry and is genuinely complex-linear. After choosing an eigenvector in its kernel, `(0,0)` vanishes. Every other coefficient is bounded by the two largest squared singular values; this remains valid at repeated/zero singular values. The scalar estimate is an exact complex triangle/Cauchy estimate. Summing all entries yields the sum of both transformed Frobenius energies, hence factor two. The refined commutator proof separately handles zero top eigenvalue and cancels the strictly positive squared norm only in the positive branch. Thus the central refined commutator theorem is discharged internally, not hidden in Definitions or a final hypothesis.

For a square contraction the SVD entries lie in `[0,1]`. The exact values `d ± i√(1-d²)` have squared modulus one and average `d`, so the constructed two unitary matrices average the actual contraction. The unitary-middle identity and invariances give the endpoint bound; squared Frobenius averaging gives the contraction result. Finally `B=0` is split before normalization by its actual positive operator norm. No matrix inverse or rank hypothesis is used.

The rectangular reduction pads into order `m+n`, rather than the informal source's `max(m,n)`. This is a faithful alternative because the square estimate is dimension independent. Upper and lower block products are proved to recover exactly `ABC-CBA`. Norms and **all** singular values require and receive separate semantic proofs. The Gram characteristic polynomial gains a power of `X`; the full multiset of roots gains exactly the corresponding zeros. Sorting appends zeros only because all original Gram eigenvalues are nonnegative. The `getD` argument treats out-of-range indices and empty dimensions, so no truncation or dropped multiplicity occurs. The final theorem applies the square bound and transports each actual norm/value through these identities.

The fixed two-by-two example proves actual equality values, including the singular values from an identity Gram operator. It establishes the normalization and sharpness of the coefficient, while the universal proof remains independent of this example.
