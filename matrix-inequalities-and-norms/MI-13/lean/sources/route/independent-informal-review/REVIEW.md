## Mathematical checks

For the inner product tr(Y*Z), the Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint of D=ad(X) is ad(X*). Expanding gives J(Y)=[X*,Y*]=−D(Y)* and J²(Y)=−D*D(Y). Conjugate linearity sends real −1 and the real eigenvalue lambda to themselves, so TJ=JT is valid despite J not being complex-linear. Cyclic trace gives tr(Y*X*Y*)=tr(Y*Y*X*), hence Y is orthogonal to JY. On a positive eigenvalue eigenspace, `||JY||²=||DY||²=lambda||Y||²>0`; this proves JY is nonzero, and orthogonality proves complex independence. These facts fail neither for nonnormal X nor repeated singular values.

The linear functional f takes exactly the (0,0) entry of U*[X,Z]V. If f(Y)=0, Y already suffices. Otherwise `f(JY)Y−f(Y)JY` has f=0 and is nonzero; taking its inner product with JY proves nonzero directly without a separate dimension-counting theorem. Complex coefficients cause no problem because T and f are complex-linear. The vector remains in the same top eigenspace. With X=USV*, the transformed commutator is SP−QS for P=V*ZV and Q=U*ZU. The canceled coordinate is indeed the only pair whose coefficient can be 2*s1² rather than at most s1²+s2². Every other pair is bounded by that latter sum, including i=j>0. Complex two-coordinate Cauchy–Schwarz and nonnegative summation therefore give exactly the factor two after both unitary Frobenius norms become ||Z||². Cancellation uses Z≠0; the top eigenvalue zero case is handled separately.

For a unitary W, `(AWC−CWA)W=[AW,CW]` has the correct multiplication order. A contraction's actual SVD yields unitary P diag(d±i*sqrt(1−d²)) Q*, since every d lies in [0,1]. Their average is precisely B; no inverse of B is used. Frobenius convexity then gives the same bound. Scaling occurs only after proving the actual operator norm of nonzero B is positive. B=0, rank-zero/rank-one A, zero C, complex entries, repeated/zero singular values and both rectangular dimension orderings are retained. The displayed 2×2 example has both sides four, so the proposed target's coefficient and norm conventions are consistent.

## Foundations and smaller implementation routes

The pinned Mathlib has enough finite-dimensional foundations for this plan, but there is substantial new formalization work. Its complete SingularValues file defines the actual decreasing, nonnegative, zero-extended singular-value sequence from the Gram spectrum, with square identities and support/rank semantics. It does not provide a finished full SVD, unitary singular-value invariance, operator-norm/first-singular-value identity or the required padding theorem in the inspected files/search scope. Those remain proved obligations; a structure containing their conclusions is not a substitute.

1. Use `EuclideanSpace Complex (Fin r × Fin r)` for the commutator space, with an explicit linear flattening and finite entry/trace identities. This avoids accidentally using the ordinary matrix entrywise norm. Frobenius and L2 operator norms must have separate explicit definitions or scopes; the pinned CStarAlgebra matrix source itself warns that a type ascription alone can select the wrong norm.
2. For the commutator's largest eigenvalue, `LinearMap.IsSymmetric.hasEigenvalue_iSup_of_finiteDimensional` supplies the supremum of the actual nonzero-vector Rayleigh quotients as an eigenvalue. `ContinuousLinearMap.rayleighQuotient_le_norm` bounds the quotient. This can avoid constructing an entire ordered eigenbasis on the r²-dimensional matrix space. Prove positivity/nonnegativity and the norm bound from the genuine D*D identity, and separate Y=0. The adjoint/inner-product convention must be normalized carefully, as in the existing RA02 Rayleigh bridge.
3. For full square SVD, take the ordered Gram eigenbasis vi and si=actual singularValues(i). On the subset si>0, define ui=si^-1 Xvi and prove orthonormality. `Orthonormal.exists_orthonormalBasis_extension_of_card_eq` extends that *partially indexed* family while fixing the positive indices. It requires only the ambient dimension equality; an extra explicit rank-counting development is unnecessary for this construction. At si=0, the Gram identity directly gives ||Xvi||²=0, so Xvi=0. This proves Xvi=si ui for every index, including zero matrices. Do not reuse MF07's nonsingular-only singular-coordinate theorem as if it covered this case.
4. Optional padding simplification: embed in the sum-index space `Fin m ⊕ Fin n` instead of dimension max(m,n). Put A and C in the upper-right block and B in the lower-left block. Their three-factor difference is the upper-right embedding of the original expression. `Matrix.fromBlocks_multiply` and `fromBlocks_conjTranspose` prove these identities directly. The Gram matrices become a zero block plus the original Gram matrix; `charpoly_fromBlocks_zero₁₂/zero₂₁` and the actual spectral multiplicity/order facts can establish zero padding. This avoids the m≤n/n≤m case split. The bound is dimension-independent, so using dimension m+n changes no final constant or quantifier. The preservation of ordered first-two singular values and both norms still needs proof, especially when there are fewer than two positive singular values; the block identities alone do not discharge it.

These are optional implementation optimizations, not replacements for the exact frozen target. The original max-dimension padding argument is also correct. Unit norm and singular-value invariance should be derived from actual maps/bases or full spectral multiplicity, not uniqueness of a chosen eigenbasis. Keep these facts behind opaque helper lemmas; repeatedly unfolding sorted eigenbasis definitions would create unnecessary elaboration work.
